01 · Directory

One app. Every independent wildlife site.

Visitors land here. Filter by type, tap a featured card, drop into that centre's in-app experience — no separate download.

A
Shared shell. Every centre lives inside one app. Cheap for them, discoverable for visitors.
B
Own-branded shell. Same content, different wrapper. For larger centres who want their own app.
→
Tap the featured Gauntlet card to see the visitor experience.
For Paul PWA-first. Shared shell and own-branded are the same codebase with a theme/config swap. Content per-centre in one backend.
02 · Visitor

What a visitor sees on arrival.

Four features from the deck. This is the whole visitor product surface — no bloat, no feature creep.

1
Site map — where the aviaries and displays are. Tap a station.
2
Live schedule — today's flying displays with a live NOW marker.
3
Species profiles — scan a QR at the aviary. Try Balder the eagle-owl.
4
Conservation — the story behind the centre and where donations go.
Tap any card Every feature opens its own screen. All content is real — Gauntlet's actual bird names, real conservation projects.
03 · Admin

How Emma at Gauntlet runs it.

Once onboarded, the day-to-day is a five-minute check-in. The operator sees what the visitor sees — but writable.

1
Today at a glance — live scans, on-site count, displays remaining.
2
Tap 14:00 to move a display. The change goes live to every visitor on site.
3
Push a note — "Balder is out today at 15:00" straight to phones of everyone on site.
The five-minute rule If it takes Emma more than 5 minutes a day to run this, the model breaks. Every admin decision is scoped to that constraint.

Meet Balder

Balder joined us in 2019 from a private collection whose owner could no longer care for him. He's Grace's favourite to fly — bold, curious, and often stars in our afternoon displays. Listen out for his call at dusk; it carries for over a mile.

In the wild

The largest owl species in the world. Found across Europe and Asia, from coastal cliffs to boreal forest. A breeding pair defends a territory of up to 15 km². Prey ranges from voles to young roe deer.
