Wildlife App Prototype · v0.1
For Paul · 18 Jul
01 · Directory

One app. Every independent wildlife site.

Visitors arrive here first. Search or browse the map, tap a centre, and drop into their in-app experience — no separate download.

Model A — every centre shares one shell. Cheap for them, discoverable for visitors.
Model B — a centre wants their own branded app? Same content, different wrapper.
Try tapping the amber pin — that's Gauntlet.
For Paul. PWA-first, so the "shared shell" and "own-branded shell" are the same codebase with a theme/config swap. Content per-centre lives in the same backend.
02 · Visitor · at Gauntlet

What a visitor sees on arrival.

Four features from the deck — nothing more. This is the whole visitor product surface.

Site map — where the aviaries and displays are. Tap a station for detail.
Live schedule — today's flying displays, with a live "now" marker.
Species profiles — scan a QR at the aviary or browse. Try the Eurasian Eagle-Owl.
Conservation — the story behind the centre and where donations go.
Tap any tile to open its screen. Everything's real content from Gauntlet Birds of Prey.
03 · Admin · Gauntlet's side

How Emma at Gauntlet runs it.

Once a centre is onboarded, day-to-day operation is a five-minute check-in. This is the operator's view — the same content the visitor sees, but writable.

Today at a glance — scans, displays scheduled, notifications queued.
Edit the schedule — tap a display to move or cancel it (weather, keeper off sick).
Push a note — "Balder is out today, come and see him at 14:00." Live to every visitor on site.
The porfell test. If it takes Emma more than 5 mins a day to run this, the model breaks. Everything else in admin flows from that constraint.

About Balder

Balder came to Gauntlet from a private collection in 2019 when his previous owner could no longer care for him. He's Grace's favourite to work with — bold, curious, and often stars in our flying displays. Listen out for his call at dusk; you can hear it from over a mile away.

In the wild

The largest owl species in the world. Found across Europe and Asia, from coastal cliffs to boreal forest. A pair defends a territory of up to 15 km². Prey ranges from voles to young roe deer.

Our work & where donations go

Every ticket funds a bird that couldn't survive on its own.

Since 1988, Gauntlet has taken in raptors from private owners, illegal trade, and injury. 40% of the collection are rescues.
